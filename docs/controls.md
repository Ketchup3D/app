Orbiting always uses the combined bounding-box center of all objects, including hidden objects, or the origin in an empty model. Orbiting preserves the current framing and zoom after panning or zooming; the model center stays at its current viewport position. Shift may be pressed or released during an orbit gesture. Panning changes the camera position and target together without changing camera orientation. Temporary middle-button navigation preserves the active tool and unfinished line. While using Line, Rectangle, Circle, Polygon, or Freehand, holding Ctrl or Command makes cursor movement orbit without holding a mouse button. Releasing the modifier restores drawing without placing a point; unfinished geometry, axis locks, and typed lengths are preserved. Holding Shift also pans during this temporary orbit.

Typed lengths accept `mm`, `cm`, `m`, `km`, `in`, `ft`, and `yd` (or `"` for inches and `'` for feet), with optional spaces before the unit. Unit names are case-insensitive. A number without a suffix still means meters. Rectangles take two independent lengths separated by a comma: `5m,50mm` creates a 5 m by 0.05 m rectangle, while `5,0.05` creates the same size. Choose a first corner and move toward the desired quadrant before entering dimensions. Circles and polygons use the typed length as a radius; Freehand scales the stroke uniformly around its start to the typed total path length. Move, Push/Pull, and Offset share the same length syntax, including signed distances. Scale continues to use a unitless factor. Enter confirms the typed dimensions; invalid input keeps the operation available for correction.

Dimension inputs also accept formulas with `+`, `-`, `*`, `/`, and parentheses. Units are converted before evaluating: `60cm - 89mm` is `0.511m` (511 mm), `(60cm - 89mm) / 2` is `0.2555m`, and `2 * 5cm` is `0.1m`. Multiplication and division take precedence over addition and subtraction. Plain numbers default to meters when added to lengths and act as unitless multipliers or divisors in products. A unit can follow a parenthesized numeric expression, such as `(60 - 8.9)cm`. Rectangles accept a formula for each dimension, for example `6m - 100cm,60mm - 10mm`. Formulas work for drawing lengths, radii, total freehand length, Move, Push/Pull, and Offset; Scale accepts formulas containing only numbers. Invalid formulas, division by zero, and area or volume results leave the operation available for correction.

Hovering an existing endpoint, midpoint, or position on an edge acquires that position as an axis reference. As the pointer moves away, it snaps near the red X, green Y, or blue Z axis through that reference. A colored dashed line and a marker at the reference show the active alignment. This works before the first drawing point, while drawing lines or shapes, and while placing measurement points. Direct geometry snaps keep priority. With an arrow-key axis lock active, hovering the reference directly sets the endpoint coordinate on that axis; the cursor can stay over the reference edge even when the endpoint is far away on screen. For example, with Y locked, the endpoint takes the hovered point’s Y coordinate while keeping the start’s X and Z coordinates. Visible geometry inside groups, nested groups, and components can supply this reference, including geometry outside the group currently being edited. A dashed connection shows the projection from the hovered geometry to the endpoint. Explicitly locked drawing planes and snapped surfaces are preserved; unlocked shapes can turn their plane to follow a reference axis. Turning off Snap to edge or Snap to axes releases the reference, as does hiding or deleting its source geometry. Hover another point or edge to replace the reference.

Rectangle, Circle, Polygon, and Freehand support arrow-key drawing-plane locks before or after the first point. Right locks the YZ plane (red X normal), Left locks XZ (green Y normal), and Up locks XY (blue Z normal), always through the first point. The preview uses the locked axis color. Repeating the arrow or pressing Down unlocks the plane and resumes automatic inference from the original surface orientation. Switching locked planes during a planar freehand stroke preserves the sampled stroke’s shape and size. Typed dimensions, reference snapping within the plane, cancellation, and undo work with locked shapes. Plane locks remain available when geometry snapping or Snap to axes is off.

Coordinates use meters and Z-up. Free drawing starts on Z=0, or at a snapped existing point/surface. Subsequent free segments use the horizontal plane through their start; axis inference and locks allow drawing vertically. The Snap menu has three independent geometry options, all enabled by default: Snap to edge attracts drawing to visible edges, including endpoints and midpoints; Snap to face targets filled surfaces; Snap to face center targets the area center of a visible face. There is no geometry master switch. Each option is remembered across reloads and restarts. Older settings with Snap to objects disabled migrate to all three geometry options disabled. Snap to axes, also checked by default, automatically aligns drawing directions with the red X, green Y, and blue Z axes at every point in a connected chain, including short segments. Points can also snap onto the visible model axes. Automatic alignment combines with geometry snapping, allowing perpendicular previews to acquire nearby endpoints, edges, and surfaces; explicit arrow-key locks keep points strictly on the chosen axis. The live endpoint marker and dashed axis guide show the snapped position before clicking; the same position is used when confirming the point. These options are independent; arrow-key axis locks remain available even with automatic axis snapping off. Move also combines intersecting axis guides from visible endpoints and midpoints: a target can align along X with one point and Y with another, or along all three axes. Colored dashed guides and an alignment label show the combined target before confirmation. Snap to edge and Snap to axes must both be enabled to use these references; arrow-key locks remain strict.

Close a flat outline (for example, four lines making a rectangle) to generate a filled face. Concave, vertical, and tilted planar outlines work too. Non-planar or self-crossing outlines remain edges. Snapping a new endpoint onto an existing edge splits it into connected edges; a boundary-to-boundary divider splits a face. Arbitrary crossings without a snapped endpoint do not split automatically. Line creation uses click-to-click input. Rectangle, Circle, and Polygon show a live outline before the second click and create a filled face. These shapes and Freehand begin on the plane of the face under the starting point, or a horizontal plane through that point. Without an explicit lock, they snap to visible points, edges, and axes in three dimensions and adjust the working plane to include the target. Existing compatible planes stay unchanged; off-plane targets choose a matching world plane or a tilted plane through the first point. Circles and polygons keep a true circular radius, and rectangles keep perpendicular sides. Freehand preserves earlier samples when acquiring a target on another plane, allowing strokes in three dimensions. Explicit arrow-key plane locks keep all subsequent targets on the selected plane. Freehand samples the drag into connected edges; returning to its start with Snap to edge enabled closes the outline. Each shape or stroke is one undo step; Escape or switching tools cancels its preview.

Click a face interior to select its fill; click near a visible edge to select the edge instead. Faces and edges can be selected together with Shift or box selection. Deleting a face leaves the boundary edges intact, and unrelated edits do not regenerate the fill. Retrace a boundary edge to heal an erased face. Nested outlines create independently selectable inner faces; deleting an inner fill leaves a real hole. Faces use depth-tested, double-sided rendering, and the inspector shows selected surface area.

Push/Pull extrudes a face with a cap and side walls, including walls around holes. Pulling an existing solid face stretches its adjoining walls. Positive typed distances pull toward the camera-facing side; negative distances push away. Scale uses the absolute value of the factor around the selection center (`2` or `-2` doubles it, `0.5` or `-0.5` halves it). Dragging past the center also uses the factor’s magnitude. Zero is rejected because it would collapse the geometry. With no lock, free pointer movement scales uniformly using radial distance in the view, without restricting the pointer to a model axis. One axis lock scales only that axis, and two locks scale only their plane. Arrow keys and toolbar locks work before and during scaling; Down clears all locks. Pointer scaling follows the edge, face, face-center, axis, and grid snapping settings. Exact object targets take precedence over the grid, and typed factors take precedence over pointer snapping. Move and Scale also work on groups. Move-copy preserves the original geometry and selects the placed copy. After placing it, `/3` creates copies at one-third, two-thirds, and the end of the original span; `3x` creates three copies at one, two, and three times the original displacement. Counts can be revised repeatedly (1–1000) until the next operation; the whole array is one undo/redo step. Press Escape to end array entry while keeping placed copies. Connected vertices move together, and changes that would bend a planar face or collapse an edge are rejected. Push/Pull and Offset can use the only face in a selected group or component, including nested groups, without opening it. Groups with multiple faces require opening the group to choose a face.

Offset adds parallel outlines in the face plane: positive distances inset the outer boundary and expand holes, while negative distances extend the outer boundary and shrink holes. Offset follows the edge, face, face-center, axis, and grid snapping settings. Arrow keys and toolbar locks constrain its measurement point to an axis or a plane through the starting point, before or during the operation; Down clears the locks. The signed distance is measured inward from the starting point, and the outline stays in the face plane. Exact geometry targets take priority over the grid, and typed distances take priority over pointer snapping. Collapsed or crossing offsets are rejected. Paint offers preset swatches and a custom color picker. The new operations support undo/redo and autosave. Move, Scale, Push/Pull, and Offset preview the snapped anchor before the first click. Move, Scale, Push/Pull, and Offset keep the chosen anchor visible during the operation, including while entering a distance or with guides hidden. Their live snap markers use the same shapes, colors, and labels as drawing tools, and their cursor icons sit 15 pixels away from the snap point, as with Dimensions. Face anchors follow the geometry, axis, and grid snapping settings while remaining on the chosen face; exact geometry targets take priority over the grid. Push/Pull and Offset also show their anchor over empty space, using the drawing plane and snapping settings, or a cursor marker when no plane target exists. Starting either operation still requires an editable face. Modeling previews leave the saved geometry untouched until confirmation; Escape or switching tools cancels them.

Right-click offers Make Group, Create Component, Ungroup, Explode, Cut, Copy, Paste, Paste in Place, Delete, Hide, and Lock. Right-clicking a selected entity preserves the whole selection; clicking another entity selects it first. Unavailable actions are disabled. Groups and named components select as single objects, support nesting, and isolate their geometry from new lines. Ungroup releases groups; Explode also releases component instances, one level at a time. Move and Scale act on the whole selected object. Double-click a group or component to edit its children; double-click nested objects to continue inward. Selection, drawing, transformations, Push/Pull, Offset, deletion, grouping, and paste act within the current editing level. The breadcrumb shows the open hierarchy; click an ancestor or Model to return, press Escape to close one level, or double-click outside the open object. Escape first cancels any unfinished operation. Locked objects cannot be opened. Component copies retain a shared definition: edits to their children update every linked instance while preserving each instance’s position and scale, including components nested in other definitions. Exploding detaches that instance.

Hide removes geometry from rendering, picking, snapping, and Fit; right-click a hidden item in the Outliner and choose Unhide to restore it. Locked entities remain selectable and appear with blue edges; choose Unlock to edit them again. These changes participate in undo/redo and local saving. Clipboard copies preserve faces, holes, intentionally missing fills, colors, nested objects, and original coordinates. Copy writes a KetchUp geometry payload to the system clipboard for keyboard paste into another KetchUp window; the context menu also keeps an in-session copy. Escape cancels paste placement without changing the model.

In the Outliner, drag a group or component onto the middle of a group row to move it inside. Drop near the top or bottom of a row to reorder siblings; an insertion line shows the position. Drop beside a top-level row or in the empty space below the tree to move objects to the model root. Dragging a selected object moves all selected objects together in their displayed order. Groups open after 500 ms of continuous hover. Moves and ordering preserve object placement, support undo/redo, and are saved with the model; Escape cancels the drag. Locked objects and recursive nesting are protected. Changes inside linked components update their other instances.

Tape Measure and Protractor start in Create Guides mode. Tap Ctrl or Command to toggle Measure only; the mode is remembered across tools, reloads, and restarts. Tape Measure offsets an infinite dashed guide parallel to the starting edge (including its midpoint); starting at an endpoint or a free point creates a guide point at the destination. Protractor creates an infinite angled guide through its center. Type a distance or angle and press Enter to place precisely, or adjust the most recently placed guide. Guides save with the project, support undo/redo, and provide snap targets for drawing, including guide intersections. View → Guides controls visibility and snapping; Edit → Delete Guides removes all construction guides with undo support. Guides do not split edges or create faces. Measure only shows the true 3D distance or the smaller angle (0–180°) between rays without changing the project. Results stay visible until another measurement, a tool change, or Escape. Middle-button navigation preserves unfinished measurements.

Dimensions adds persistent linear annotations with extension lines, endpoint ticks, and a meter label. The third click places the dimension away from the measured geometry. During placement, Right / Left / Up locks the dimension line and its displayed length to X / Y / Z while preserving both picked endpoints; repeat the arrow or press Down to return to the full aligned length. If the locked axis is perpendicular to the measured span (for example, Z for two points on a horizontal face), placement moves the extension lines along that axis and preserves the full measured length instead of collapsing the dimension. The saved dimension keeps this alignment when the source geometry changes. Snapped endpoints attach to vertices, edges, or surfaces and update when that geometry moves or scales; deleting the source geometry keeps the annotation at its last position. Free points remain in world coordinates. Select a dimension by its line or label to delete, move, copy, group, or hide it. Dimensions support undo/redo, local saving, and component editing.

The toolbar is ordered Select, Line, Erase, Orbit, Push/Pull, Scale, Offset, Move, Paint, Dimensions, and Union. Dimensions groups Dimensions, Tape Measure, and Protractor, with Dimensions selected by default. Every flyout entry has an icon, and each group remembers its last chosen tool.

Hover over a toolbar tool or flyout entry for one second to see its name, keyboard shortcut (when available), and usage instructions.

The **Union** flyout keeps Flip, Combine, Union, Intersect, Trim, and Split together. Flip mirrors the selection through its bounds center using a colored plane, an inspector axis button, or the X/Y/Z arrow shortcuts. Mirrored component instances retain their links and placement when their definition is edited.

Boolean tools work on closed solid groups/components or separate connected loose bodies. Nested groups and components are combined as volumes, including overlapping child solids. Fill missing faces and remove stray/internal edges first. For Union, Combine, Intersect, or Split, either select two bodies before choosing the tool, or click the first and second body. For Trim, click the cutter first and the target second; the cutter stays. Escape cancels the first-body choice.

Intersect also accepts a face or open surface group/component against a closed body, in either order, and keeps the portion of the surface inside or on the body. Two coplanar faces or surface groups/components keep their shared area. These surface intersections replace both inputs with an editable surface group and preserve face colors; they do not require the result to enclose volume.

Union combines volumes while retaining enclosed voids. Combine also removes surfaces around fully enclosed voids; through-holes remain open. Intersect keeps shared volume for two closed bodies. Split produces separate groups for the overlap and each body's remainder, omitting empty pieces. Union, Combine, Intersect, and Split replace their two input bodies; Trim replaces only the target. Results are editable groups with planar faces and preserved face colors. Operations support undo/redo, local saving, and scoped group/component editing. Locked or excessively complex bodies, unsupported open bodies, and unrepresentable results are rejected without modifying the model.
